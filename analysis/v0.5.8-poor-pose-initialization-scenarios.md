# v0.5.8-poor-pose-initialization-scenarios

## Request

Clarify:

```text
Are poor pose initialization cases common in practice?
What real scenarios produce them?
```

## Short Answer

Yes, but not in the simplistic sense of:

- every frame,
- every dataset,
- or always with catastrophic `100 deg+` orientation error.

The more accurate statement is:

- mild to moderate poor pose initialization is common;
- severe poor pose initialization is common in failure, recovery, or degenerate-geometry regimes;
- and those are exactly the regimes where nonlinear BA behavior becomes most scientifically interesting.

Also, in practice "poor initialization" often means:

- not totally random,
- but wrong enough to put a local nonlinear solver into the wrong basin.

That distinction is important.

## Why It Is Common Enough To Matter

Most real systems do **not** solve every BA problem from scratch.

Usually they are warm-started from:

- the previous frame/window estimate,
- IMU integration,
- PnP,
- essential matrix decomposition,
- GPS/INS priors,
- relocalization results,
- or map alignment.

So in easy steady-state tracking, pose initialization is often decent.

But practical systems still repeatedly hit windows where initialization quality degrades enough to matter.

That is why "bad initialization" is not a corner case in research terms, even if it is not the default every frame.

## Typical Real Scenarios

### 1. Tracking Loss And Reinitialization

This is one of the most common real sources of poor pose initialization.

After tracking loss:

- feature tracks are broken;
- correspondences may be sparse or partially wrong;
- the restarted pose is often only coarse;
- local BA can begin in a much worse basin than during normal tracking.

This is especially relevant for:

- monocular VO / SLAM,
- visual relocalization,
- UAV flight with temporary blur or rapid turn,
- handheld motion with sudden occlusion.

## 2. Low-Parallax Forward Motion

Forward-looking motion with short baseline is a classic weak-geometry regime.

Examples:

- UAV flying forward,
- body camera walking forward,
- vehicle-mounted forward camera,
- endoscope / tunnel / corridor motion.

In these cases:

- translation, depth, and rotation are more strongly coupled;
- many different pose/structure explanations produce similar image motion;
- a moderately poor initialization can be enough to destabilize direct joint BA.

## 3. Repetitive Texture Or Weak Visual Uniqueness

Some scenes give a plausible but wrong initialization because the visual evidence is ambiguous.

Examples:

- building facades with repeated windows,
- office corridors,
- warehouse shelves,
- crop rows,
- tiled floors or structured ceilings.

Then:

- feature matching may be locally self-consistent but globally wrong;
- PnP or relative pose can still produce a plausible pose;
- BA starts from a basin that is already biased.

## 4. Dynamic Objects And Partial Outlier Contamination

Even if the robust loss is decent, a coarse initializer built from contaminated matches can already be damaged.

Examples:

- cars, pedestrians, crowds,
- foliage,
- propellers or moving rig parts,
- temporary occluders.

The point is not only gross failure.

A more realistic issue is:

- enough outliers survive to shift the initial pose,
- and then the nonlinear solver must decide whether to correct that shift or absorb it through coupled variables.

## 5. Weak Or Coarse Relocalization Priors

Many systems use:

- image retrieval,
- place recognition,
- map-based coarse alignment,
- or GPS/INS orientation priors.

Those priors are often useful but not precision-grade.

So a BA window can start with:

- translation approximately right,
- gravity approximately right,
- but the in-plane or viewing-direction-related rotation still noticeably wrong.

That is especially close to the kinds of stress axes explored in this repository.

## 6. Cross-Strip / Cross-Pass Aerial Matching

In aerial or photogrammetric blocks, difficult initialization can happen when:

- strips have different look directions,
- cross-strip overlap is limited,
- oblique views create stronger appearance change,
- or GNSS/INS is only coarse.

Then the pose prior may be good enough to be plausible, but still not good enough for direct coupled BA to behave cleanly.

This is particularly relevant to the repository's Exp5 interpretation.

## 7. Calibration / Synchronization / Extrinsic Imperfections

A pose initializer can look poor even when the matching is not the main problem, because the model behind it is slightly wrong.

Examples:

- time offset,
- rolling-shutter effects,
- approximate intrinsics,
- imperfect camera-IMU extrinsics,
- lens-model mismatch.

Then:

- the "best" initial pose under the imperfect model may already be biased;
- BA starts from a pose that is not random, but still wrong enough to matter.

## 8. Wide Nonconvex BA With Coupled States

Poor pose initialization matters most when the optimization is strongly coupled.

Examples:

- pose + inverse depth,
- pose + translation,
- pose + free `XYZ`,
- monocular local BA,
- sparse-view bundle adjustment.

In those cases, the danger is often not:

- "the initial pose is absurdly bad"

but:

- "the initial pose is wrong enough that the optimizer can explain the residual using the wrong variables."

That is exactly the regime this repository is targeting.

## How Common Is "Very Bad" Initialization Really?

Three levels are helpful.

### Mild Error

Very common.

- normal tracking drift,
- noisy motion prior,
- imperfect PnP.

### Moderate Error

Common in hard windows.

- weak geometry,
- partial outliers,
- relocalization,
- degraded overlap.

### Extreme Error

Less common as a routine event, but realistic as a stress-test proxy.

- after tracking collapse,
- after wrong coarse association,
- after severe blur,
- after aggressive recovery.

So if a repository uses very large initialization errors, that should usually be read as:

- a controlled probe of the nonlinear basin structure,

not as a literal claim about everyday frame-to-frame operation.

## Best Practical Interpretation For This Project

The strongest real-world reading is not:

```text
real systems always have terrible pose initialization
```

It is:

```text
real systems often encounter windows where pose initialization is
good enough to start optimization,
but still poor enough that coupled BA can enter the wrong basin
unless the search is structured carefully
```

That is a much more realistic and defensible claim.

## Selected Q&A

### Q1

Is poor pose initialization mostly a recovery problem rather than a steady-state problem?

### A1

That is a very good practical summary.

Steady-state tracking often has decent warm starts. The most important bad-init regimes are:

- recovery,
- relocalization,
- degenerate geometry,
- ambiguous appearance,
- and strongly coupled BA windows.

### Q2

Does the repository need catastrophic initialization failure to justify the staged story?

### A2

No.

The key issue is not total randomness. It is:

- wrong enough to enter the wrong basin,
- especially when other variables can absorb the pose error.
