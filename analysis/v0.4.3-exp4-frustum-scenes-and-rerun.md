## Request

- increase camera spacing;
- visualize cameras as frustums instead of only center markers;
- regenerate the scene figures first;
- then rerun Experiment 4 and check whether the staged-polar story still holds.

## What Changed

### Scene Geometry

Experiment 4 camera trajectories were redesigned to look more like forward-moving BA windows:

- `local` path length increased from about `0.91 m` to about `3.86 m`;
- `global` path length increased from about `2.37 m` to about `9.79 m`;
- pose rotations were also made milder and more realistic, instead of the earlier stronger per-view twisting;
- point-depth ranges were moved farther out so the enlarged camera path still sees a stable front-facing scene.

### Visualization

`save_free_xyz_scene_plot(...)` now renders each camera as a frustum:

- the frustum corners are derived from `equidistant_unproject(...)` of the image corners, so the shape is tied to the actual fisheye camera model rather than a made-up pinhole wedge;
- fixed reference views use gray frustums;
- optimized later views use purple frustums;
- the camera path remains a dashed polyline;
- plotting limits use robust point-cloud percentiles so a few far outliers do not shrink the frustums into unreadable dots.

## Interpretation

### Does the Story Still Hold?

Yes.

After enlarging the camera baselines and making the scene figures more BA-like, the main qualitative conclusion did not change:

- direct joint BA still often falls into bad basins under large yaw initialization error;
- staged polar initialization still consistently rescues pose before full joint BA;
- the staged method still reaches `100%` convergence in both `local` and `global` regimes in this experiment.

### What Changed Relative to the Earlier Exp4 Version?

The local regime became noticeably easier **after** the pose basin is fixed:

- local structure RMSE improved from about `0.252` to about `0.082`;
- local translation error also improved substantially for the staged method.

This is expected. Once the local baseline stopped being tiny, the short-window BA had much more real geometric leverage.

### Local vs Global Difference Now

The local/global story still exists, but it shifted slightly:

- `local BA` is no longer an almost-degenerate tiny-baseline case, so once staged initialization fixes pose, local structure can now refine fairly well;
- `global BA` still does better, with lower staged structure error (`0.055` vs `0.082`) and a more stable final basin across all yaw levels;
- so the difference is now less "local structure remains weak no matter what" and more "global still wins, but local is no longer artificially too weak."

That is a healthier result than before, because it suggests the earlier local weakness was partly geometric, not purely algorithmic.

## Best Current Reading

This updated Experiment 4 supports a stronger and cleaner claim:

- the staged theta/phi idea is not just rescuing an unrealistically tiny-baseline toy setup;
- it still works when the camera motion looks more like an actual BA trajectory with meter-level spacing and visible view frustums;
- the story is therefore more credibly about bad-basin avoidance in joint pose-structure optimization, not only about one extreme synthetic geometry.

## Limitations

- all tracks still survive the full window, so visibility dropout and occlusion are still missing;
- the first two views remain fixed, so this is still not a fully free global-similarity BA;
- the scene is more realistic in camera layout, but still denser and cleaner than a real SLAM / SfM track graph.

The biggest change was in the `local BA` regime. Once the baseline became more realistic, local free-`XYZ` structure recovery became much better after staged pose rescue.
