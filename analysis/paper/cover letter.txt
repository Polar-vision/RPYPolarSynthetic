Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript entitled “A Decision Rule Without an Operating Characteristic: The Pairwise Sum in Multi-Channel Boresight Alignment” ​ for publication as a regular paper in IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement.

Multi-channel payloads are allocated alignment tolerances one channel at a time but verified one pair at a time, through the sum of two allocations. This paper asks what that pairwise check actually establishes. We show that, in design, the rule is optimistic by a fixed constant (allocations derived from pairwise sums are 15.5% too small, with the worst case being three channels in an equilateral configuration — a consequence of Jung's theorem), and that, in assembly, the rule has no operating characteristic of its own: its rejection region is contained in the region already rejected by the per-channel check, so no build record can distinguish it from an exact feasibility test. We bound the depth of this blind spot and reproduce the analysis on a public ten-channel specification, recovering its arithmetic to the arc-second.

The paper contributes directly to the theory and methodology of measurement: it is a study of a conformance-assessment decision rule — how a tolerance table converts measured deviations into an accept/reject verdict, and what evidence could ever validate such a rule. We believe it fits the journal's scope on measurement theory and uncertainty and should interest readers working on calibration, tolerance budgeting, and instrument alignment.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the submission and declare no conflict of interest.
