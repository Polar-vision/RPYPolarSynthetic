# v0.4.4-exp4-track-dropout-occlusion

## Request

Continue improving Experiment 4 by adding more realistic `track dropout / occlusion`.

## Motivation

In `v0.4.3`, the camera motion and frustum visualization were already much more realistic, but the track graph was still too clean:

- every local point was visible in all `5` views;
- every global point was visible in all `8` views;
- there were no mid-track gaps;
- so the BA geometry looked better, but the observation graph was still unrealistically dense.

This version makes the track graph more like real BA by adding:

- view-to-view track termination;
- structured occlusion in later views;
- variable track lengths;
- occasional missing observations inside a track span.

## What Changed

### 1. Track-End Dropout

Each geometrically visible point now receives a stochastic `track end` in the later views.

Design:

- the first two fixed reference views must still stay visible, because the initial 3D points are triangulated from them;
- after that, the point does not automatically survive to the end of the window;
- farther and more central points are more likely to survive longer;
- closer or more off-axis points are more likely to terminate earlier.

So this is not pure random deletion. It is biased by the point's anchor-view depth and bearing, which makes the resulting dropout more physically plausible.

### 2. Ray-Box Occlusion

Local and global scenes now include world-space occluder boxes.

A later-view observation is removed if the segment from the camera center to the point intersects one of these boxes before reaching the point.

That produces:

- structured missing observations;
- occasional gaps inside a track span;
- a more interpretable reason for why some landmarks disappear in specific views.

### 3. Scene Metadata

Experiment 4 scenes now explicitly store:

- `occluder_boxes_world`
- `gap_counts`

so the new observation graph can be summarized and visualized directly.

### 4. Scene Visualization

The Experiment 4 3D scene figures now draw the occluders as translucent amber boxes.

This makes the dropout / occlusion mechanism visible in the same figure as:

- the landmark cloud,
- the camera path,
- the camera frustums.

## Comparison To v0.4.3

After adding dropout / occlusion:

- the staged method still stays at `100%` convergence in both regimes;
- the direct joint methods become clearly less reliable, especially in `global BA`;
- staged final structure quality becomes worse than the full-visibility case, which is expected because the observation graph is now sparser and less redundant.

Main changes:

- local staged structure RMSE: about `0.082 -> 0.124`
- global staged structure RMSE: about `0.055 -> 0.081`
- local observations: `600 -> 439`
- global observations: `1280 -> 954`

So the new track graph is meaningfully harder, not just cosmetically different.

## Interpretation

### Does The Main Story Still Hold?

Yes.

Even with:

- variable track lengths,
- missing later-view observations,
- explicit occluders,
- and fewer total measurements,

the staged theta/phi initialization still robustly rescues the optimizer into a good basin before full joint BA.

### What Changed In The Story?

The story is now more credible as a BA claim, because the observation graph is no longer unrealistically complete.

The updated interpretation is:

- `staged polar` is still primarily a **bad-basin avoidance** mechanism;
- its value survives even when the track graph is sparser and less idealized;
- the price of realism is that final structure accuracy becomes worse than in the full-visibility version, even for the staged method.

That is actually a healthier result than before, because it shows the method is not winning only because the graph is unrealistically dense.

### Local vs Global Difference Now

The local/global gap is still present, but it is subtler than in earlier Exp4 versions:

- `local BA` remains shorter and more weakly constrained, with staged structure RMSE around `0.124`;
- `global BA` still benefits from the larger window and reaches staged structure RMSE around `0.081`;
- once occlusion and dropout are introduced, both regimes become harder, but `global BA` retains a clear redundancy advantage.

So the current best reading is:

- the staged story still holds in both local and global synthetic BA;
- global BA still turns rescued pose into better structure than local BA;
- but the difference is no longer being inflated by an unrealistically full track graph.

## Selected Q&A

### Q1

Did adding dropout / occlusion break staged initialization?

### A1

No.

It made the problem harder and reduced final structure accuracy, but staged still converged in all tested trials.

### Q2

What became less realistic in the old version, and what is better now?

### A2

The old version already had more realistic camera motion, but its observation graph was too perfect. The new version is better because:

- not every point survives the full window;
- some tracks have interior gaps;
- occluders provide a geometric explanation for missing observations.

### Q3

Is this already a fully realistic BA benchmark?

### A3

Not yet.

- the first two views are still fixed;
- outlier correspondence handling is not the focus here;
- the occlusion model is still synthetic and box-based.

But this version is materially closer to a real BA track graph than the full-visibility one.
