## Selected Q&A

### Q1

In `ba_scene_3d.png`, why are there only 4 black `x` markers? Does that mean there are only 4 mismatches between the target view and the anchor view?

### A1

Yes, in the default Experiment 2 configuration there are 4 synthetic outliers in the target-view correspondences.

- The scene uses `72` points and `outlier_fraction = 0.06`.
- The implementation computes `round(72 * 0.06) = 4`.
- These outliers are created by shuffling the second-view observations only, so the anchor-view side is not corrupted in the same way.

### Q2

Why do the faint gray rays not all connect to every point?

### A2

This is only a visualization choice to keep the figure readable.

- The plotting code draws only a uniformly sampled subset of anchor rays.
- In the current implementation the cap is `max_rays = 18`.
- The missing rays do not indicate missing data or failed correspondences.

### Q3

What does "anchor-view inverse-depth anchoring" mean in this experiment?

### A3

It means each 3D point is parameterized from the anchor camera by a fixed viewing direction plus one depth variable, instead of optimizing a fully free `XYZ` point.

- The anchor view provides a bearing direction `anchor_bearing`.
- The world point is reconstructed as `point_world = anchor_bearing * depth`.
- The optimized scalar is stored as `log_inv_depth`, then converted back to depth during evaluation.
- Geometrically, the point is "anchored" to an anchor-view ray and mainly slides forward/backward along that ray when depth changes.

### Q4

Why does Experiment 2 optimize only each point's `log_inv_depth` instead of a full free `XYZ` landmark?

### A4

This is a deliberate design choice for both geometric correctness and optimization stability.

- In the current Experiment 2 formulation, the anchor view already fixes each landmark's viewing ray through `anchor_bearings`.
- The point is reconstructed as `point_world = anchor_bearing * depth`, so once the ray is fixed, the remaining per-point unknown is only the distance along that ray.
- If the implementation switched to free `XYZ` landmarks without also adding anchor-view residuals, the problem would become underconstrained because the current BA residuals only use the second-view observations.
- Even if anchor-view residuals were added, free `XYZ` would still introduce two extra lateral degrees of freedom per point that the anchor-view ray is supposed to rule out.
- Those extra freedoms would let structure absorb pose errors more easily, which would weaken the intended pose-depth-coupling stress test.
- This is closely related to the staged-design comment in the solver: when depth-like variables are too free, they can absorb tilt errors and destroy the geometric decoupling the experiment is trying to test.

Using `log_inv_depth` instead of raw depth also helps numerically.

- It keeps the landmark state to one scalar per point instead of three.
- Inverse depth is a standard monocular BA parameterization because distant points are usually better behaved in inverse-depth space than in raw depth.
- The `log` form keeps the parameter strictly positive after exponentiation and makes optimization over a wide depth range smoother.

### Q5

The current story is built around pose-depth coupling. If all landmarks were free `XYZ` variables instead, would the same story still hold? And if there are many tracks, not just one track, does the anchor-ray argument still matter?

### A5

Broadly yes, but not in exactly the same form.

What still holds:

- Once landmarks are optimized jointly with camera pose, the problem still has strong pose-structure coupling.
- In fact, with free `XYZ` landmarks the coupling is usually even stronger, because each point now has three movable coordinates instead of one scalar depth-like variable.
- So the high-level motivation for staged or rotation-first initialization can still remain valid.

What no longer holds exactly:

- The current Experiment 2 story relies on a hard anchor-view parameterization: each track has a fixed anchor bearing and only slides along that ray.
- With anchored inverse depth, the point update is effectively one-dimensional along its anchor ray.
- With free `XYZ`, the point can also move laterally away from that ray, so the clean staged-decoupling story becomes weaker.
- In that case the current theta-only / phi-only explanation is no longer an exact geometric statement about the full BA state.

Why multiple tracks do not remove the issue by themselves:

- The anchor-ray argument is indeed per track, but in a multi-track scene you simply have many anchor rays, one per landmark.
- If you use anchored inverse depth, each landmark remains constrained to its own ray exactly by parameterization.
- If you use free `XYZ`, those per-track ray constraints become soft measurement constraints instead of hard state constraints.
- More tracks improve the total amount of information, but they also introduce more free structure variables.
- So "many tracks" does not automatically eliminate the extra structure freedom; what matters is parallax, view diversity, noise level, and how structure is parameterized or marginalized.

The right rephrasing is:

- With anchored inverse depth, the story is specifically about pose-depth coupling.
- With free `XYZ`, the story becomes a more general pose-structure coupling story.
- The intuition that direct joint BA can fall into a bad basin may still be true, but the exact Experiment 2 mechanism should not be claimed unchanged without a new experiment.
